# Story-by-story evaluation

Scores use the same five-point rubric as the Model E report. Prompt fidelity
measures whether supplied facts remain true; entity tracking covers names,
roles, pronouns, and ownership; causal coherence measures whether events follow
from one another; fluency covers readability and repetition. Fixed-length
truncation is not heavily penalized.

## Individual notes

- **open_story / focused:** This is Model F's best result. Lily, her mother, and
  the dog remain distinct, and the danger-to-safety sequence forms a complete
  miniature story. The mother thanking Lily and calling her kind has no setup,
  the group goes inside twice, and the moral contradicts the claim that the dog
  was harmless, but the narrative remains easy to follow.
- **open_story / creative:** Mittens remains the protagonist, but rock, tree,
  flight, weight, and climbing attributes merge. A tree becomes a rock, then a
  friend, and the sentence about playing at the top is malformed. The output
  has a story-shaped cadence without a stable world model.
- **named_entity / focused:** Fluffy is preserved strongly at first, and Tweety
  is introduced as the helper. The ending then overwrites every participant
  with Fluffy: Fluffy thanks Fluffy, answers Fluffy, and becomes friends with
  Fluffy. This is a direct entity-binding failure.
- **named_entity / creative:** Fluffy remains central, but the helper changes
  from Freddy to Teddy and pronouns lose their referents. A falling tree becomes
  emotional and the motivation shifts between finding a home, keeping healthy,
  and avoiding sadness without a causal bridge.
- **object_ownership / focused:** Ben initially receives the red ball, but the
  story falls into a near-verbatim dog/dialogue loop. Speaker and actor roles
  repeatedly collapse into "the dog," and ownership is never resolved.
- **object_ownership / creative:** Ben retains the ball longer and Lily remains
  present, making this the stronger ownership sample. It still reverses the
  transfer in dialogue, uses ambiguous "he," and duplicates the dog into both
  aggressor and target.
- **two_characters / focused:** Mia and Tom remain distinct, but the supplied
  kite and boat disappear immediately. Repeated climbing and repeated rocks
  replace meaningful interaction or progression.
- **two_characters / creative:** The kite remains relevant, but its ownership is
  inverted when Mia asks Tom for "your kite." The initial boat is discarded,
  Sam appears without introduction, and the closing conflict contradicts the
  earlier agreement to play together.
- **persistent_goal / focused:** The food promise and deadline are abandoned.
  The fox chases and climbs instead, while the rabbit largely disappears. The
  final regret gestures toward failing the rabbit but does not recover the
  promised task.
- **persistent_goal / creative:** This is the weakest sample. Fox and rabbit
  actions blend through hopping pronouns, the food is forgotten, the deadline
  has no consequence, and the story becomes an unrelated lake excursion.
- **dialogue_location / focused:** Emma reaches a tree, but Noah and the planned
  meeting disappear. The tree becomes both a speaking tree and a wise owl,
  calls Emma "Lily," and then enters repetitive dialogue with her.
- **dialogue_location / creative:** The old oak tree remains in the text, but
  Emma avoids it rather than waiting there, Noah vanishes, and the setting
  drifts from backyard to park. A generic toy-discovery story replaces the
  rendezvous.

## Aggregate assessment

The focused profile averages approximately **2.1/5**, and the creative profile
approximately **2.2/5**. Model F produces its strongest gains on unconstrained
openings: the focused Lily story is more complete and readable than most
earlier-model samples. The controlled prompts expose the remaining limitation.
Names and objects can remain lexically present while their relationships,
ownership, promises, and locations are overwritten.

Compared with Model E, Model F's story-validation loss improves from `2.0030`
to `1.8752` and perplexity from `7.41` to `6.52`, but the qualitative suite
does not show a corresponding improvement in relational consistency. The extra
depth appears to improve local prediction and story-shaped prose more reliably
than persistent state tracking.
