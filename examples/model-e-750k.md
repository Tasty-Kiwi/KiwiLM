# Story-by-story evaluation

Scores use the same five-point rubric as the Model E and Model F reports.
Prompt fidelity measures whether supplied facts remain true; entity tracking
covers names, roles, pronouns, and ownership; causal coherence measures whether
events follow from one another; fluency covers readability and repetition.
Fixed-length truncation is not heavily penalized.

## Individual notes

- **open_story / focused:** Lily, her mother, the dog, and the ball remain
  distinct, and the story reaches a clean resolution. Lily inexplicably
  apologizes for breaking a ball that the dog took, so the replacement-ball
  solution does not follow from the stated problem. It is nevertheless one of
  the model's strongest complete stories.
- **open_story / creative:** Timmy and his mother remain stable, but the feared
  friend, rain, and reason for going outside never form one causal thread.
  "Safe from you" and "made him up" are malformed, while the ending recasts his
  mother as his brave friend.
- **named_entity / focused:** Fluffy remains lexically dominant but not
  referentially stable. The kind lady starts speaking as Fluffy, Fluffy promises
  to help Fluffy, and the protagonist eventually cannot find Fluffy. The nest
  problem appears without setup.
- **named_entity / creative:** Kitty is introduced and immediately replaced by
  Spot, "the bird," and duplicated Fluffys. Toy ownership and speaker identity
  cannot be recovered, although the prose retains a generic helping moral.
- **object_ownership / focused:** Ben initially receives and keeps the red ball,
  which is better than most ownership outputs. The story then abandons the
  object test for a truck/man sequence in which the vehicle, man, and speaker
  inherit one another's attributes.
- **object_ownership / creative:** Ben's red ball remains present, but additional
  blue and yellow balls appear and their ownership changes within individual
  sentences. Pronouns and actions such as "He took the blue ball to Ben" make
  the transfer impossible to track.
- **two_characters / focused:** The opening immediately assigns Mia's kite to
  Tom, reversing the prompt. It then collapses into a repeated "give it back"
  loop, while the red boat disappears completely.
- **two_characters / creative:** Mia and Tom remain present and the lake provides
  a plausible destination for the boat. The boat is then forgotten, Mia's
  mother appears ambiguously, and a rock replaces both original objects. This
  is still the stronger two-character sample.
- **persistent_goal / focused:** Food, the promise, and the deadline disappear
  after the prompt. Singing, a noise, and the fox flying away replace the
  requested task, and the ending duplicates the fox.
- **persistent_goal / creative:** The output is short and relatively fluent but
  semantically disconnected. The rabbit acquires wings, food becomes a tree,
  and neither delivery nor deadline is represented.
- **dialogue_location / focused:** Noah and the oak tree disappear immediately.
  A cloud becomes Emma's conversational partner, Emma addresses herself, and
  repeated friendship statements lose both speaker identity and meaning.
- **dialogue_location / creative:** The oak tree is retained and eventually
  revisited, but Noah is replaced by Sue. A door appears in the tree, locations
  drift to the park, and the promised rendezvous never occurs.

## Aggregate assessment

The focused profile averages approximately **2.0/5**, and the creative profile
approximately **2.2/5**. Model E 750k produces credible unconstrained openings
and sometimes preserves simple possession for several sentences. It still
fails the suite's central relational tests: participant identity, ownership,
promises, deadlines, and rendezvous locations.

The matched-data ablation separates Model F's gains cleanly. Original Model E
had story-validation loss `2.0030`; additional data and targets reduce that to
`1.9357`, while Model F reaches `1.8752`. Thus the matched Model E run recovers
about **53%** of the original Model E-to-F loss improvement, and Model F's added
capacity accounts for the remaining **47%** under this training setup.

Model F retains the raw-likelihood lead, but not a decisive qualitative lead on
these twelve samples. Model E 750k trains about 47% more valid tokens per second
and finishes roughly 31% sooner, making it the stronger quality-per-compute
result.
