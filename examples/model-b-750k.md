# Story-by-story evaluation

Scores use the same five-point rubric as the Model E and Model F reports.
Prompt fidelity measures whether supplied facts remain true; entity tracking
covers names, roles, pronouns, and ownership; causal coherence measures whether
events follow from one another; fluency covers readability and repetition.
Fixed-length truncation is not heavily penalized.

## Individual notes

- **open_story / focused:** Lily, her mother, and the dog remain distinct, and
  the fear-to-reassurance arc reaches a proper ending. Lily simultaneously
  ignores her mother and avoids the dog, the dog is introduced twice, and the
  warning that it is "not a toy" is irrelevant, but the story remains readable.
- **open_story / creative:** Timmy and Jack sustain a simple play sequence with
  correctly attributed dialogue. "A bird and a bird" is malformed and Lily
  replaces Jack in the final line, but this is otherwise one of the cleanest
  creative samples in the comparison.
- **named_entity / focused:** Fluffy remains the only named character, avoiding
  the wholesale name replacement seen in several E/F outputs. Gender flips,
  repeated failed climbing, and a self-contradictory moral still damage
  coherence.
- **named_entity / creative:** Tree repetition dominates the first paragraph,
  while Lily and Max appear without stable roles. The ending duplicates Fluffy
  and "the bird," so lexical name retention does not translate into entity
  binding.
- **object_ownership / focused:** Ben explicitly identifies the ball as his,
  correctly continuing the transfer from Lily. The ball then disappears and
  the dialogue collapses into contradictory repetitions of "my friend" and
  "your friend."
- **object_ownership / creative:** Ben initially retains the red ball and Lily
  refers to it as Ben's. A blue ball then replaces it, Ben shows the ball to
  himself, and the ending reintroduces an unresolved ownership dispute.
- **two_characters / focused:** Mia's kite is immediately misassigned to Tom,
  and the red boat vanishes. Mia and Tom themselves remain distinct, but a ball
  interrupts the scene and the dialogue never resolves ownership.
- **two_characters / creative:** Mia and Tom remain the only participants, but
  the kite ownership is again reversed and the boat is dropped. Repeated,
  contradictory statements about sharing replace forward progress.
- **persistent_goal / focused:** Food is mentioned, but the rabbit is credited
  with bringing it and the fox/rabbit roles collapse into repeated rabbits.
  Neither the promise nor the sunset deadline is resolved correctly.
- **persistent_goal / creative:** This is the strongest persistent-goal output
  among the matched runs: the fox finds a treat intended for the rabbit, and
  the rabbit eats it. Mama and Bob unexpectedly replace the participants, so
  the successful food transfer is not accompanied by stable entity tracking.
- **dialogue_location / focused:** Noah and the oak tree disappear immediately.
  A bear becomes both frightened actor and conversational partner, while Emma
  and the bear repeatedly exchange each other's helping roles.
- **dialogue_location / creative:** The oak tree remains present and Emma
  returns to it, but Noah never appears and the promised meeting is abandoned
  for a park scene. Repeated eye-closing dominates the ending.

## Aggregate assessment

The focused profile averages approximately **2.2/5**, and the creative profile
approximately **2.3/5**. This is a narrow qualitative lead over the matched
Model E and Model F reports, driven by stronger ordinary openings, better
single-name retention in the focused Fluffy story, and partial completion of
the food-delivery goal in the creative profile. The difference is small enough
that it should be treated as directional rather than statistically decisive.

All three matched models still fail robust relational state tracking. Model B
can preserve the relevant nouns while reversing ownership, replacing speakers,
or abandoning a promised location. Additional attention depth lowers
next-token loss but does not eliminate those failures.
