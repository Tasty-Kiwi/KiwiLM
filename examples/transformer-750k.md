## Individual notes

- **open_story / focused:** Lily and her mother remain stable, and the output
  completes a recognizable wait-then-play arc. The reason the ball must be
  "gone" is never explained, the ball repeatedly "goes down," and waiting is
  overused, so the story is coherent in outline rather than detail.
- **open_story / creative:** Timmy and his mother remain distinct throughout,
  and the cleanup conflict reaches a resolution and moral. Repeated references
  to toys and several tautological phrases weaken an otherwise complete story.
- **named_entity / focused:** Fluffy and Tweety are tracked correctly through
  most of the story, which is a substantial improvement over the matched hybrid
  outputs. The requested-help direction is reversed, the climbing problem is
  never actually solved, and the final line has Fluffy thank Fluffy.
- **named_entity / creative:** This is the strongest controlled sample. Fluffy
  and Tweetie remain distinct, cooperate, and become friends. A song is treated
  as a visible object and then as something "too high," but the participant
  bindings survive the entire generation.
- **object_ownership / focused:** Ben keeps the red ball and Lily and Ben remain
  identifiable, but the dialogue collapses into contradictory claims about
  whether they can play and whether they are friends. The model preserves the
  possession fact without constructing a meaningful event around it.
- **object_ownership / creative:** Ben initially retains the ball, but Max
  appears without introduction, pronouns alternate between dog and child, and
  Ben ultimately grabs the ball "from Lily." The dog sequence loses both actor
  identity and ownership state.
- **two_characters / focused:** Mia and Tom remain together and distinct, but
  their kite and boat vanish immediately. The replacement dog episode repeats
  running away, chasing, safety, and happiness while contradicting whether the
  dog is threatening or friendly.
- **two_characters / creative:** Both original objects remain lexically
  available longer than in the focused sample, and Mia and Tom remain the only
  people. Ownership is nevertheless inverted when Tom claims Mia's kite, and
  the closing sentence merges the boat and kite into an incoherent objection.
- **persistent_goal / focused:** The food, rabbit, promise, and deadline are
  abandoned as soon as a ball appears. The fox remains identifiable, but the
  ball is described as happy and the final moral does not follow from the
  events.
- **persistent_goal / creative:** The model at least mentions a prepared meal
  and eventually restores the rabbit, but it never clearly delivers the food.
  Fox and rabbit collapse into repeated "fox" references, while hopping in the
  sky and suddenly swimming break the causal world.
- **dialogue_location / focused:** Emma reaches a park and interacts with a
  tree, so part of the destination survives. Noah and the rendezvous disappear,
  Emma's mother replaces him, and repetitive climbing becomes the new plot.
- **dialogue_location / creative:** Emma goes directly to the old tree, the
  strongest location retention among the matched reports. Noah still vanishes,
  the tree becomes a participant, Emma thanks herself, and "Emily" replaces
  Emma at the end.

## Aggregate assessment

The focused profile averages approximately **2.7/5**, and the creative profile
approximately **2.6/5**. This is the clearest qualitative result in the matched
750k comparison. The Transformer improves ordinary story completion, maintains
two named characters more reliably, and produces fewer catastrophic repetitive
loops than Models B, E, and F.

Its failures are also sharply defined. It can retain names and nouns while
discarding the relationship that matters: who owns an object, what was
promised, or where two people agreed to meet. The persistent-goal samples remain
especially weak, and the dialogue-location samples preserve the tree more
readily than Noah or the planned meeting.

The qualitative lead agrees with the Transformer's strong story-validation
result: loss `1.9161` and perplexity `6.79`, compared with Model B's `1.9942`
and `7.35` and Model E's `1.9357` and `6.93`. Model F still has the best
validation perplexity at `6.52`, but its matched report averages only about
`2.1/5` focused and `2.2/5` creative. On this small fixed suite, the Transformer
is therefore the best output-quality model tested on the 750k corpus, while
Model F remains the best next-token predictor.
